Hey, I'm Raunaq Rai, an astrophysics PhD student at University College London, supervised by Dr. Guido Roberts-Borsani. I work with the James Webb Space Telescope, the most powerful instrument ever built for observing the first galaxies in the Universe.

The telescope has opened up the first billion years of cosmic history to detailed study for the first time, and the chemistry of the galaxies it found there is not what we expected. Many of them contain far more nitrogen, relative to oxygen, than the galaxies around us today. That matters because different stars release different elements on very different timescales, some within a few million years, others over hundreds of millions. The mix of elements in a galaxy is therefore a record of what it has already done, and finding this much nitrogen so soon after the Big Bang is hard to explain. It suggests that galaxies in the early Universe grew, and formed their stars, differently from the ones we see nearby.

Why that happens is not understood, and it is what I work on. I ask whether the nitrogen excess is a quirk of a handful of unusual galaxies or the norm across the population, and use that to constrain the mechanisms that could produce it.

Before UCL I read Natural Sciences at Durham and stayed on for an MSc in Data Science, then did an MPhil in Data Intensive Science at Cambridge.

Away from the desk you'll find me playing hockey (for Richmond Hockey Club), on the golf course or on a tennis court. I am also building Bluebell AI, a startup working to level the playing field in education. bluebellai.org
