Hey, I'm a PhD student in the Department of Physics & Astronomy at University College London, working on extragalactic astrophysics with the James Webb Space Telescope, supervised by Dr. Guido Roberts-Borsani.

I use the incredible data from the James Webb Space Telescope to look at galaxies that existed around 13 billion years ago.

The chemistry of these systems has been surprising. One of the revelations of the JWST era is nitrogen: these early galaxies contain far more of it, relative to oxygen, than we see anywhere in the local Universe. Chemistry is a record of everything a galaxy has already done, so by working out what these systems are made of we can reconstruct their formation histories and place real constraints on how the first galaxies were built. Much of my work asks how far the nitrogen result generalises: whether it belongs to a handful of unusually bright galaxies, or whether it is characteristic of the early population as a whole, and what either answer means for how these galaxies formed their stars and grew their black holes.

Before UCL I read Natural Sciences at Durham and stayed on for an MSc in Data Science, then did an MPhil in Data Intensive Science at Cambridge.

Away from the desk I am usually somewhere between a hockey pitch, a golf course and a tennis court, playing hockey for Richmond Hockey Club. I am also building Bluebell AI, a startup working to level the playing field in education. bluebellai.org
